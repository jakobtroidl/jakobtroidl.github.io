Jakob Troidl

I am an AI Scientist at HHMI Janelia, part of the AI@HHMI initiative, where I lead the MIA-AI project. We build large-scale datasets and pre-trained models for microscopy image analysis, using self-supervised and multi-modal learning and architectures that reason across spatial scales.

My background is in computer science and connectomics, the reconstruction and analysis of neural circuits from nanometer-resolution microscopy data. I’ve worked on learned models of neuron morphology and self-supervised isotropic volume reconstruction, proofreading and annotation infrastructure (CAVE, SynAnno), and tools for analyzing circuit motifs (Vimo, MoMo). I also contributed to the largest human connectome to date and a connectome of the larval zebrafish brain.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Harvard, advised by Hanspeter Pfister, with research stays in the Turaga Lab at Janelia and at E11.bio as an ML Fellow. I hold a B.Sc. and M.Sc. in Computer Science from TU Wien.
